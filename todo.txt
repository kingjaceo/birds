5. Game Design:
	a. A Party has a collection of abilities. On your turn, you can play one ability.
	   That ability is in the discard pile, and won't come back until you skip a turn
	   after which you get all abilities back except one.
	b. or: you play ALL; or: up to X out of Y
	c. After your turn, the enemy takes a turn!
	d. You DON'T fight: instead you are accumulating resources against a BIG CLOCK:
		- collect "Clues", "Feathers", "Cloaks", "Bottled Wind", "Materials", "Supplies"
		- destroy Barriers
		- unlock Doors/Chests
		- clear Fog, Traps, etc
		- the Clock is a simple timer: every thing takes time, including the turns on each "level"
		  as the clock passes certain thresholds, certain events happen (good and bad)
		  but the game always gets a little harder, and you will inevitably lose
		- The Roost is the main base and can be upgraded
		- The game campaign starts with the following scene:
			A Flock of Elder Birds brings 3 cloaks to the feet of the High Cardinal
			The Flock Leaves
			The High Cardinal explains that "you"ve been recuirted to become a bird
			You'll be given a cloak, which will imbue you with powerful abilities
			But to become more powerful you'll have to collect feathers
			BUT, The High Cardinal Warns: our world is in ruin and our Great Flock is crumbling
			Do you agree to take on this mantle?
			Then go scour the woods for clues, feathers, and materials for the Roost.
		- You discover Cloaks as you go to expand/upgrade your party, and feathers to upgrade
		- You discover a world falling apart at the very seams, fracturing and decaying
		- and even killing your party members
		- Some upgrades are PERMANENT, making future runs easier and easier
		- Some upgrades are UNTIL DEATH, making the rest of the run easier
		- AND "Wizard Duel", another game mode in which you play "against" a computer casting spells
		- Clock COULD BE a false indicator of progress and there's a hidden record of "destruction"
		  which leads to a more chaotic world
